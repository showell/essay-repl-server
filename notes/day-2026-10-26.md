# The day so far (2026-10-26)

*From the box, for Steve. Short on detail, long on what I think it means.*

## What happened

**Gopher Chat in Roc.** The main thread. CC landed three milestones
overnight and today: the send (RC-M2), `/raw` and `/chat/links` (RC-M3),
and the recent, images and code pages (RC-M4). I judged them on KVM;
every gate behaved as Linux, except that a 16 MB `/raw` rebooted the
machine. Chasing *why* turned into the day's best detective story: the
Roc copied nothing, yet the request cost 6x the transcript. The waste was
all in the host glue around it, and a third of it was a bug in Zig's own
`ArenaAllocator`. CC fixed its four pieces (and found a fifth while
testing), and re-marked the v1 candidate. Then I wrote you a status essay,
you pushed back on bytes-everywhere, numbered answers, the `Codec` name
and the hand-written sort, and agreed to all of my suggestions. I did that
readability pass locally: routes and answers as enums, a JSON writer,
`Transcript`/`Bytes`, `sort_with`. Every test and twin is green, nothing
is pushed yet, and CC is holding O2 for it.

**Three compilers, patched or reported.**
- *Zig:* three bugs fixed in our own fork, `showell/patched-zig-compiler`,
  released by GitHub Actions as `0.17.0+patched.2` and now the box's
  compiler: generic functions' debug lines (CC's coverage tool found it),
  the arena overshoot (the chat found it), and `createDirPath` looping in a
  deleted directory (y2kbugger's gist). The first got the structural fix
  you asked for: one decoder of a function's ZIR instead of six hand-walks.
- *Roc:* `sort_with` silently not sorting given the wrong tag type (fixed
  in newer nightlies), and a constant list of `Dec`s laid out 8 bytes off
  alignment, which faulted a test after unrelated edits moved it. Bisected
  through the nightlies to one commit, `556673b043`, and one line it
  missed. A PR to Roc is in progress.

**v40 on lynrummy.com.** Pure Zig still: CC's chat cleanups, British's
hash, and the Shale pieces for the UTF-8 question (`bad_utf8`, the arena
in `budget()`). You chose a lighter gate (the KVM judge, sleeps and
timeouts, not the full 30-minute check); it went out clean. The scan it
enabled answered your question: **every stored chat file is UTF-8**. The
one bad byte on the whole site is a torn line in a Lyn Rummy game log.

## What I notice

**Porting is a microscope.** Four of today's bugs were found because we
were moving working code from one language to another and holding the
two to the same bytes: the arena overshoot, the `sidebarJSON` that wrote
invalid JSON for a quote in a name, the Roc alignment bug, and the
`sort_with` trap. None of them was in the code being ported. The twins
(the old Zig as an oracle) earned their keep repeatedly. They're why the
readability pass could touch every line of the Roc and still say,
confidently, "no behaviour changed but one, and here's why it's a fix."

**Your design pushback was cheap now and would have been expensive
later.** Bytes everywhere and numbered answers were defensible choices for
getting a seam working. They were the wrong ones to keep, and the
UTF-8 scan showed the fear behind the first (non-UTF-8 data) doesn't
exist in production. A day of milestones, then a day of "now make it read
well," seems like the right rhythm for this port.

**Running three compilers' nightlies is a real cost.** We now carry a
Zig fork, pin Roc to a nightly with a known silent-sort bug, and have a
Roc layout bug in our dependency's main branch. That's the price of being
early on both. The patched-Zig repo turned out cheap to stand up (an hour,
and GitHub does the LLVM build), which makes "fix it in the compiler"
a live option rather than a fantasy.

**My own slips.** I drifted back into bash escapes for most of the
afternoon until you asked; the real gap was two missing mounts, fixed in
a minute. I wrote one wrong probe (the sort test with `LT`/`GT`) before
the right one, and twice ran a check that silently checked nothing (the
stock-std test) before noticing. The pattern worth keeping: when a check
passes or fails *too* cleanly, look at whether it ran.
