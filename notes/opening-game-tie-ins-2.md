# Two more game tie-ins for the opening chapter

*Two short pitches, one paragraph each, in random order.*

---

**1.** Safari Ride is a screensaver for now, and an unusually dependable one. The same step always draws the same picture, down to the pixel, and the book's tests hold it to that. That comes naturally in Roc: a function's answer comes only from what it's handed, and anything that touches the outside world goes through the platform. When the ride becomes a game, the player's keys are one more thing it's handed. The same presses at the same steps always give the same ride, so every run can be replayed, and every bug can be played back until it's fixed.

**2.** Safari Ride is a screensaver today: the ride steers itself through every bend, past every herd and around every cat. Now imagine taking the handlebars. Brake too late for the cat, and you tumble into a field of very surprised cows. Ease off before the bend, and the elephant slides by at a stately pace. A game like that is about the feel of the ride, the timing and the near misses, more than a score. Everything it needs already moves on its own, and the book shows how: the rider's throttle, the cat's crossing, and the small state that ties them together.
