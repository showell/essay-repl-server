  - The first, playing notramo, said `--flat` is a fine bridge but the wrong shape for a Roc-native linter.
  - The second found that my "use roc-ast as an oracle" idea already existed in Luke's repo, and that his oracle missed a real span bug, now issue #101.

  The lesson is in how they were briefed: blind, with fuzzy direction and permission to say the premise was wrong.

## What went wrong, and what stuck

- **I stalled the box.** To push a comparison past a memory budget I raised it to 12 GB on an 8 GB machine with no swap, which turned the guard off. You then made the right general call: on the box, budgets serve the job, not the other way round, but the machine's real limits still bind. That's in Shale's docs now, and in memory.
- **A release shipped without its headline item.** v46 went out with the Cobblestone apps still in it, because the work that removed them was done but never merged and I didn't check the release's list against what was actually merged. v47 fixes it and waits for your import.
- **One script ignored a failed step.** A failed fast-forward let the next steps verify the wrong tree, and I told Cloud something was merged when it wasn't. I caught and corrected it within minutes, but it's the same class of mistake as the stall: a guard I didn't respect.
- **Running things one at a time paid off.** Timing gates got the box to themselves, comparisons ran in chunks, and every claim of "identical" came with a count. Twice that discipline turned up old bugs, not new ones.

## Biases worth naming

You asked whether we're still shaped by bare metal, or just by the length of the session. Both, I think.
- **Bare metal:** the budgets and the habit of keeping Shale's build free of dependencies come from bare metal. Those are right for the server and over-applied on the box.
- **Shale as the universal shell:** this made roc-ast's output text for Shale, when the more useful product for anyone else is the tree itself.
- **The long session:** I proposed an oracle that already existed, and only a fresh agent found that out.

**What to keep:** Shale for acting on code, roc-ast as the compiler's ground truth. **What to reconsider:** roc-parser's typed trees for anything written in Roc. Cloud's overnight experiment should tell us which recipes could move and what they'd lose.
