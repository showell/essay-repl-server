# Anton's feedback: an action plan

**What he said** (#contributing > a little Roc book): "I like the prose and tone :) One thing that comes to mind, for those who are unfamiliar with animation, it could increase cognitive load because they are learning both animation and Roc at the same time."

**What you answered:** the introduction should say plainly that the reader learns two things.

Here's the plan, smallest first. Steps 1 and 2 are cheap and could land today. Step 3 is a check that tells us whether steps 4 and 5 are needed at all.

## 1. Say it in the introduction

One short paragraph in "Introduction": this book teaches two things at once, Roc and how a ride like this one is animated, and it keeps the second one light. The animation is a handful of plain ideas: a frame is a picture, a step moves the world on, far things look small, and near things cover far ones. The math behind them waits in one chapter near the end. Readers who know animation can skim those parts, and readers who know neither get both at a walking pace.

That paragraph is a promise (C12), so step 3 checks that the book keeps it.

## 2. Count animation ideas as new ideas

MISSION C13 already caps a paragraph at two new Roc ideas. Make it two new ideas of either sort, Roc or animation. A paragraph that brings in a type variable and perspective at once is the load Anton means. It's a one-line change to MISSION.

## 3. Audit the ten chapters for it

One cold agent reads the ten chapters as a reader who knows some Roc and no animation. It tags each new idea as Roc or animation, and lists:

- the paragraphs where both kinds arrive together;
- the animation ideas used before they're explained;
- anywhere the math shows through.

Then it ranks the hot spots. That gives us evidence, not guesses. My guess is that "The problem", "The pieces as types" ("The rider's view") and "A far tree" (outlines, the screen's `y`) carry the most.

## 4. Fix the hot spots, one chapter at a time

The fix follows the loop's usual rules: subtraction first (C13), the picture before the details (H7), and the animation idea in plain words before the Roc that holds it (D5). Chapters you've already read get the lightest touch.

## 5. A standing reader for it

Add the "no animation" reader to each chapter's cold readers, beside the truth-and-order reader. Its brief is the same as step 3's, for one chapter. That keeps Anton's concern checked as the book grows, the way the negatives lint keeps D2.

## My recommendation

Do 1 and 2 now, run 3 in the background, and decide on 4 and 5 when its report is in. A reply to Anton can wait until step 1 is live, so the thread gets something concrete: "the introduction now says so; thanks."
